Completely monotone functions in general and some applications

(JMAA, 2026)

The subject of this paper is completely monotone and related functions. First of all, we recall some definitions.

Definitions

Definition 1. A function $f:I\to \mathbf{R}$, where $I\subseteq\mathbf{R}$ is an interval, is called completely monotone if $f$ has derivatives of all orders and satisfies
$$ (-1)^n f^{(n)}(x) \geq 0 \quad (n=0,1,2,\ldots;\, x\in I). $$
Definition 2. A positive function $f:I\to \mathbf{R}$, where $I\subseteq\mathbf{R}$ is an interval, is called logarithmically completely monotone if $f$ has derivatives of all orders and satisfies
$$ (-1)^n (\log\circ f)^{(n)}(x) \geq 0\quad (n=1,2,\ldots;\, x\in I). $$
Definition 3. A function $f:I\to \mathbf{R}$, where $I\subseteq\mathbf{R}$ is an interval, is called absolutely monotone if $f$ has derivatives of all orders and satisfies
$$ f^{(n)}(x) \geq 0\quad (n=0,1,2,\ldots;\, x\in I). $$
Definition 4. A function $f:I\to \mathbf{R}$, where $I\subseteq\mathbf{R}$ is an interval, is called a Bernstein function if $f\geq 0$, and $f'$ is completely monotone.

Motivations

The main motivation of this paper is to address certain inaccuracies in the existing literature, specifically regarding results published in JMAA. Our objectives are as follows:

  • Correcting the proof of Theorem 1 and Lemma 1 in [2].
  • Providing simpler proofs and generalizations for Theorem 2 in [10] and Theorem 2 in [2].
  • Generalizing Theorem 3 in [2].

In [2] Theorem 1, it is claimed that the function $f_{\alpha}(x)$ is strictly completely monotonic on $(-a,\infty)$ if and only if $\alpha \leq 1$. While the "if" part is correct, the proof of the "only if" part is flawed due to an incorrect calculation of $f_{\alpha}'(x)$.

Correction and Improvement: We cannot verify the original "only if" statement, but we provide the necessary condition $\alpha \leq 2$ in Theorem 17. Furthermore, under the condition $-1-2a+2b > 0$, we establish $\alpha < 2$. In cases where $\beta < b-a$, we successfully prove an "if and only if $\alpha \leq 1$" statement, generalizing the original theorem.
Open Problem. Let $f_{\alpha}(x)=(x+a)^{\alpha}\left[ \psi(x+b)-\psi(x+a)-\frac{b-a}{x+a}\right]$. Determine the value of $\alpha_0$ such that $f_{\alpha}(x)$ is strictly completely monotone on $(-a, \infty)$ if and only if $\alpha \leq \alpha_0$. Given our results, it is known that $1 \leq \alpha_0 \leq 2$.
